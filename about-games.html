The Foundation

We can relate to one another in an uncountable number of ways. Accordingly, there is a great variety of Relating Games. Nonetheless, there are some basic aspects that show up in all games in one way or another. And each of these aspects can also be taken directly into focus in games designed for that purpose. Below you find brief explanations of the two basic aspects Presencing and Curiosity along with corresponding games.

Presencing

Presencing is grounded attention in the moment. To be in touch with our bodies, thoughts, feelings, and reactions. In Playful Relating, we aim to be aware of ourselves and what we perceive from others on a moment-to-moment basis. This allows us to become more finely tuned to the movements within ourselves and the interaction and respond with more clarity and freedom.

The Noticing Game

Noticing is a very simple game best suited for pairs or trios. The idea is simple. Find a comfortable position to rest with your eyes closed (e.g. lying on your backs with the heads close to one another). Then you take turns completing sentences starting with "I notice ...", filling in the rest with a sensation, a thought, an emotion, and so on. Try to keep each sentence brief and simple (e.g. "I notice the back of my head resting on the floor." Play for as long as you like.

You may also want to experiment with differnt variations. What happens when you open your eyes? What happens when you take a different position in space? What happens if you restrict your noticing to only physical sensations?

Curiosity

Curiosity is all about opening yourself up to what attracts your attention and interest in the current moment.

The Curiosity Game

Curiosity is a very simple game best suited for pairs or small groups. The idea is simple. You take turns asking questions to each other. It's important that you only ask a question, if you feel genuine curiosity towards it. This is not a small-talk game! When you're asking a question, take time. It might need a moment for you to connect to your inner curiosity. Once you've asked the question, the person to whom the question was addressed may answer it, if they want to (feel free to reject a question!).

This game can turn out quite different depending on how you take turns. The simplest option is to simply alternate. But it's also interesting to put a timer and have only one person ask (or be asked) questions for that duration. Then switch.
